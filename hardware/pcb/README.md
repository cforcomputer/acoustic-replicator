# TB-25 transducer board — ordering from JLCPCB

Twenty boards are needed (18 + 2 spares). Everything in this folder is generated by `python -m replicator.pcbroute hardware/pcb` and checked by `validation/v6_pcb.py`.

## What to upload

| JLCPCB asks for | File |
|---|---|
| Gerber files | zip the whole `gerbers/` folder (10 Gerber files, 2 drill files) |
| BOM | `TB-25_BOM_JLCPCB.csv` |
| CPL (pick and place) | `TB-25_CPL_JLCPCB.csv` |

## Order settings

| Setting | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Layers | 4 | L1 ground pour, L2 +V_DRIVE plane with a +3V3 island, L3 signals, L4 parts and signals |
| Dimensions | 58 × 58 mm, 1.6 mm thick | |
| Inner / outer copper | 0.5 oz / 1 oz (their default) | |
| Surface finish | ENIG | the RP2040 is a 0.4 mm pitch QFN; HASL is uneven at that pitch |
| Via covering | tented | no via has a mask opening |
| Minimum track / gap used | 0.15 mm / 0.135 mm | inside their standard capability |
| Smallest hole | 0.3 mm (via), pad 0.6 mm | |
| Assembly side | **bottom** only | the 25 transducers on the top are soldered by hand afterwards |
| Parts | 68 placed per board, 21 part numbers, 8 of them "extended" (C2040, C131025, C20625731, C171845, C2879853, C160405, C160403, C160352) | every part number was read from JLCPCB's parts library on 2026-11-10 and was in stock; the footprints in the Gerbers are the ones in that library |

Not fitted by the assembler: R3 (120 Ω, 0603). Solder one by hand on the last board of each of the four chains.

## Check these in JLCPCB's preview before paying

1. **Rotation of bottom-side parts.** Tools disagree on how a rotation is written for the bottom side. The parts at 90° or 270° are the ones to look at: U1 (RP2040), U2 (flash), J5, and some small parts. Every polarised part has a pin-1 dot on the silkscreen; the preview must show pin 1 of the part on that dot. Correct it in their placement editor if not.
2. That all 8 extended parts are still in stock (the microphone had 113 pieces on 2026-11-10).
3. Their automatic design-for-manufacture report. My own checks are listed below; theirs is the one that counts.

## What was checked here, and what was not

Checked (`validation/v6_pcb.py`, all pass):

- every pad of the netlist is connected and nothing else is: the copper was rebuilt from the Gerber and drill files alone at 0.02 mm resolution and compared with the netlist (120 nets, 367 pads, 0 opens, 0 shorts);
- clearance between different nets ≥ 0.127 mm on all four layers (smallest found: 0.135 mm on L4, 0.14 mm on L3, 0.18 mm on L1, 0.30 mm on L2); copper ≥ 0.4 mm from the edge; holes ≥ 0.5 mm apart;
- the checker finds an open, a short and a clearance fault when I inject them;
- an independent Gerber reader (gerbonara) identifies all layers, the outline and the 290 holes.

Not checked, and not claimed:

- **No board has been built.** There is no firmware yet for the RP2040 (PIO program for the 25 phase-shifted outputs, RS-485 protocol, microphone capture).
- Signal integrity, thermal behaviour and EMC were not analysed. At 40 kHz with millimetre-scale currents I do not expect trouble; that is an expectation, not a result.
- The AMS1117 regulator is specified by its maker with a tantalum output capacitor; here it has 2 × 10 µF ceramic. This is common practice and usually stable. If the 3.3 V rail oscillates, add 1 Ω in series with one capacitor.
- The RS-485 driver is enabled from the transmit line through one transistor (no spare pin). It needs bias resistors on the bus at the hub and limits the bus to about 250 kbaud.

## Bring-up

1. Before soldering transducers: power +5 V on J1 pin 1, expect 3.3 V on the LED and 1.1 V on C10.
2. Program through J5 with a Raspberry Pi Debug Probe (its 3-pin cable fits; pin order SWCLK, GND, SWDIO). There is no USB connector.
3. Solder the 25 transducers from the back. The driven lead goes to the pad marked with a bar on the top silkscreen; if the can is connected to one lead, that lead goes to the other (ground) pad. The four leads next to the RP2040 (T13, T18) are 0.5 mm from its pads: use a fine tip.
4. Channel map (which GPIO drives which transducer): `TB-25.json`, key `channel_map`. The 25 drive pins are GPIO4–28, so one PIO `out pins, 25` with base 4 writes them all.
